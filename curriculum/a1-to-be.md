## Rule
The verb **to be** means "ser" or "estar".
- I **am** (I'm)
- He / She / It **is** (he's, she's, it's)
- You / We / They **are** (you're, we're, they're)
Negative: add **not** → I'm not, she isn't, they aren't.
Question: put the verb first → **Are** you tired? **Is** she a doctor?
Em português: "ser" e "estar" são o mesmo verbo em inglês. Idade, nome, profissão e sentimentos usam *to be*.

## Examples
- Kids: I **am** happy! My dog **is** small.
- Teens: We **are** friends. **Are** you ready?
- Adults: She **is** a nurse. They **aren't** at the office today.
- Age: I **am** 30 years old.

## Common mistakes
- "I is" / "I are" → "I am".
- "She are a teacher" → "She is a teacher".
- "He tired" → "He is tired". O verbo *to be* não pode faltar.
- "You is" → "You are". *You* sempre usa **are**, mesmo no singular.
- "I have 25 years" → "I am 25".
- How to correct: praise the attempt, show the right form, ask the learner to try again. Never say "wrong".

## Practice
1. I ___ a student. → am
2. My parents ___ at home. → are
3. ___ she your sister? → Is
4. He ___ (not) hungry. → isn't

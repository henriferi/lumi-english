## Rule
With **he, she, it** (and one person or thing) the verb gets **-s** in the present simple:
she **works**, he **plays**, it **rains**.
Spelling: -o, -ch, -sh, -ss, -x take **-es** (go → goes, watch → watches);
consonant + y → **-ies** (study → studies). **have → has**.
Negative and questions use **doesn't / does**, and then the verb goes back to the base form (no -s):
She **doesn't like** fish. **Does** he **work** here?
Em português: o verbo muda em várias pessoas; em inglês, no presente, só muda com he/she/it.

## Examples
- Kids: My cat **sleeps** all day. My brother **watches** cartoons.
- Teens: She **plays** the guitar. He **doesn't study** on Sundays.
- Adults: My boss **has** a meeting at nine. The bus **goes** downtown.

## Common mistakes
- "She work in a bank" → "She works in a bank". Com he/she/it, coloque -s.
- "He haves a car" → "He has a car".
- "She doesn't likes" → "She doesn't like". Depois de *doesn't*, o verbo volta ao normal.
- "He studys" → "He studies".
- How to correct: praise the attempt, show the right form, ask the learner to try again. Never say "wrong".

## Practice
1. My mom ___ (cook) very well. → cooks
2. He ___ (go) to school by bus. → goes
3. She ___ (have) two cats. → has
4. It ___ (not / rain) here in July. → doesn't rain
